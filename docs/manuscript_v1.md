# აი (Ai): A One-Phoneme Minimalist Constructed Language
## Phonemic Design, Georgian Script Integration, and Typological Foundations

**Manuscript draft v1 — for peer review only**
**Author:** J. Tqemaladze
**Date:** 2026-08-10

---

### Abstract

This paper presents **აი (Ai)**, a constructed minimalist language whose defining design constraint is that every word is exactly one phoneme, written as one symbol of the Georgian script (Mkhedruli). The language has 14 phonemes — identical to Toki Pona (Lang, 2014) — and a lexicon of 14 semantic roots derived from a subjective selection of "primitive" concepts. Grammar is isolating with strict SVO order and direct concatenation of modifiers. We propose that the one-phoneme-per-word constraint, combined with systematic composition, can achieve adequate expressivity while minimizing phonological and orthographic load. We situate აი within the typology of minimal phoneme inventories (11–14 phonemes; Everett, 2005; Robinson, 2006; Maddieson, 1984) and compare its design with Toki Pona. Limitations of the approach — including absence of empirical validation — are discussed.

### 1. Introduction

Constructed languages (conlangs) with deliberately reduced phonological inventories test the limits of minimalism in human communication. Toki Pona (Lang, 2014), with 14 phonemes and approximately 120 words, is the best-known example, and has been the subject of scientific attention (Colombo et al., 2024; Blahuš, 2011). The present work introduces აი, which extends the minimalist program to its logical endpoint: **one word = one phoneme = one symbol**.

### 2. Background: Minimal Phoneme Inventories

Cross-linguistic surveys (Maddieson, 1984; UPSID) place the world's smallest inventories at 11 phonemes: Pirahã (Everett, 2005) and Rotokas (Robinson, 2006; Firchow & Firchow, 1969). Hawaiian has 13 (Parker Jones, 2018). Toki Pona's 14 phonemes are thus typologically plausible. Georgian (Shosted & Chikovani, 2006) has 33 letters/29 phonemes including ejectives — a rich consonant inventory that serves as the orthographic base for აი.

### 3. Design of აი

**3.1 Phonology.** 9 consonants /p t k s m n l j w/ and 5 vowels /a e i o u/, following Toki Pona's inventory; syllable structure (C)V(N); stress initial; no tones. Georgian mapping: პ ტ კ ს მ ნ ლ ჲ უ̆ + ა ე ი ო უ; /j/→ჲ (historical letter), /w/→უ̆ (breve combination).

**3.2 Lexicon.** 14 roots selected "intuitively": existence, self, other, speech, deixis, good, bad, negation, human, thing, time, place, knowledge, quantity. No formal criterion for primitiveness is given.

**3.3 Grammar.** SVO; modifier concatenation (ვბ = good human); negation by დ before predicate; questions by VS inversion; imperatives by predicate alone. Word separator: the zero-sign **0** (pause); sentence end: **00**.

**3.4 Numerals.** Diphthong signs: Æ=1, Ꜷ=2, Ǣ=3, Œ=4, Ꝏ=5 … 272 one-symbol numerals.

### 4. Comparison with Toki Pona

| | Toki Pona | აი |
|---|---|---|
| Dictionary | ~120–140 | 14 |
| Phonemes | 14 | 14 |
| Symbols/word | 1–3 | 1 |
| Particles | li, e, la, pi | none (positional) |
| Separator | space | 0 |

### 5. Discussion and Limitations

The one-phoneme constraint trivially limits the closed-class lexicon; expressivity depends entirely on composition. We do not provide empirical data on learnability, communication efficiency, or cross-cultural transparency of the roots. The selection of the 14 roots is not theory-driven. Numerals by diphthong signs lack a positional system and are ad hoc. No computational model is provided.

### 6. Conclusion

აი demonstrates that a 14-phoneme, 14-root language is constructible; whether it is learnable or expressive remains an open empirical question.
