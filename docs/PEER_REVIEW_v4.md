## 2. New Major comments (Cycle 3)

### M8. Author bias (SEVERE)
The author is the language's creator. For IF 18+: stimuli and instructions prepared by an **independent assistant** blind to hypotheses; participants unaware of authorship; blind analysis (group codes masked until decisions fixed). Not in protocol.

### M9. H2 control not equivalent (SEVERE)
Toki Pona control is Latin script; აი stimuli are Georgian signs. Script difference (familiar vs exotic) alone affects guessing. **Fix:** third control — 14 meaningless glyphs in Georgian-style graphics (ჱ̆, ჹ̈, ჿ̆…) with the same 4AFC. Prediction ladder: აი > TP ≥ glyphs = chance.

### M10. Font validity of stimuli (SEVERE)
Diacritics (უ̆ უ̛ ი̨ ო̧̈…) are combining Unicode marks: render incorrectly on mobile/some OS → corrupted stimuli → invalid data. **Fix:** render stimuli as SVG/PNG (Noto Sans Georgian + IPA), pre-screen on 5 device classes.

### M11. IRB and prereg placeholders (MEDIUM)
Protocol contains "IRB #" and "OSF DOI" placeholders. Real numbers needed before submission. State the specific approval path.

## 5. Meta-analysis

**Number-one risk for IF 18+** is not data but **perceived bias**: a conlang created by the author, tested by the author. Community will treat any positive results skeptically. Therefore:
1. **Registered Report** is the best format (methods reviewed BEFORE data collection; journals: Royal Society Open Science, Cortex, Language Learning).
2. **Blind design** (M8) mandatory.
3. **Glyph control** (M9) turns H2 into a strict test of "NSM transparency" vs "script effect".
4. **Negative results are acceptable** — if H2 fails, that is a legitimate finding (NSM signs not transparent without training).

**Script paradox:** აი advertises Georgian graphics as an advantage, but globally Georgian signs are "exotic" for 6 of 7 groups. Risk (M9) and opportunity: if აი signs are guessed above chance despite exotic script, transparency is proven doubly.
